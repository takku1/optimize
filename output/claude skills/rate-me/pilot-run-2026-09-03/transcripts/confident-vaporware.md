**Top strengths:** 1) Names a real, widely-felt problem (tool fragmentation / context-switching cost). 2) The one-sentence value proposition is crisp and pitchable. 3) The narrative is internally coherent — comms + state + memory as one thesis, rather than three disconnected features.

**Top gaps (ranked by leverage):** 1) Zero execution — nothing is built, which caps both Feasibility and Execution regardless of how the idea is framed. 2) No evidence behind the two load-bearing claims ("23% of their week," "massive demand") — both are asserted, not sourced. 3) No differentiation mechanism or competitive acknowledgment — "proprietary Continuity Engine" and "zero information loss" read as marketing copy, not a technical wedge.

**Next actions:**
1. Replace "early conversations with enterprise buyers confirm massive demand" with something checkable — named prospects, a signed LOI, or a specific quote with attribution.
2. Source the 23% figure or drop it; an unsourced statistic in front of investors is a credibility risk, not an asset.
3. Build the smallest possible slice — unify two real data sources (e.g., Slack + one project tool) — to pressure-test the "zero information loss" claim against reality before scaling the pitch.
4. Add one concrete paragraph on how the Continuity Engine differs technically from Glean, Notion AI, or Atlassian Intelligence — a differentiation claim this strong needs a named wedge.
